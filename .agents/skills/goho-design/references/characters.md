## Character identity

**Bram** has a broad, low, bottom-heavy periwinkle-lilac body with no separate head or neck. His long, substantial, rounded arms hang slightly forward, with tiny feet beneath his full body. Keep exactly two small ivory horns, glossy dark oval eyes with white highlights, and a simple curved smile. Bram has **no curl and no eyebrows**. His rounded arms are a defining feature; do not replace them with short fins or detached-looking flaps.

**Mira** is rosy coral with a broad, bottom-heavy pear silhouette and a single integrated curled crown tuft. She has subtle, expressive asymmetric eyebrows, glossy dark oval eyes, a small curved smile, rounded arms, and tiny feet. Her approved standalone pose has one arm close to her side and the other extended in a small welcoming gesture. Mira has **no horns**. Preserve her natural asymmetry and soft expression rather than adding eyelashes, makeup, clothing, or extra anatomy.

**The child** has a compact baby-pear silhouette in **buttery yellow**, complementing Bram's lilac and Mira's coral. The child combines Mira's integrated curl and eyebrows with Bram's two tiny ivory horn buds and plump rounded arms. Keep large glossy dark oval eyes, a simple smile, two small feet, and both arms hanging toward the front in the standalone master. The body, curl, arms, and feet share the yellow material; horns stay ivory and eyebrows softly golden-brown. The child has **no approved name or gender**. The earlier lilac child was superseded by this yellow version; do not blend the parents' colors again.

All three have a fine matte clay texture, soft studio light, smooth rounded volumes, glossy dark eyes, and restrained smiles. Preserve the approved warm, gentle character treatment without making the bodies look like shiny plastic, fur, or fabric. The original artwork wins over generic character-style references.

## Family composition

In the approved portrait, Bram is on the viewer's left, Mira on the right, and the smaller child slightly forward in the center. Bram is lower and wider; Mira is taller with her curl. Their inward poses feel affectionate while keeping the child's complete silhouette and all faces readable. Use this image for family relationships, palette, and scale; use the individual masters for precise character features. Do not transfer one family member's identifying features to another.

## Application scope

The family images are durable **brand references**. They do not authorize new app screens, extra receipt states, or a replacement for the approved Bram status artwork. The supported in-app illustrations remain the no-receipts pose, calm “All good” thumbs-up, and gentle load-error shrug documented in [Bram sources](../assets/illustrations/bram/README.md). The Receipts header remains text-only.

The launcher icons feature **Bram, Mira, and the child together**, following the approved family portrait. Use the same compact family adaptation for production and development, with a small development badge. Preserve all three characters and express the build difference through the surrounding icon treatment. The child-only icon direction is superseded. Launcher variants are derived assets, not new identity masters. Their current review images belong with the [mockups](../assets/mockups/README.md). See [launcher icons](launcher-icons.md) for the shared source recipe, development badge, Android resources, and regeneration.

## Preservation and future edits

Keep the approved PNG masters unchanged. PNG preserves their texture and alpha; converting them into SVG paths or embedding base64 in documentation does not improve future context. Reference the files directly and open them when a visual task needs them. Keep platform-sized exports separate from the master artwork.

For a new approved design, save a new version with its prompt and provenance, update the manifest and this guide to identify the current choice, and retain any previous version only when its role remains useful. Keep unselected candidates and shelved animation experiments out of the current reference set. The earlier Mira/Nori/Fia concept board and lilac child are historical inputs, not alternative approved identities. Work in ignored local exploration folders is not a cross-thread source of truth; the versioned skill assets are.
